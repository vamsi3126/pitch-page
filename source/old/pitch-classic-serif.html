This is how most labs design a quantum chip today, step by step. One: an engineer writes the chip as code, hundreds of lines of Python, often in Qiskit Metal, with no picture of what they are drawing. Two: the geometry is exported into CAD files and cleaned up by hand. Three: the model is rebuilt in a separate 3D solver like Ansys HFSS or Q3D, where a single run can block a workstation for days. Four: the results are copied into Excel or MATLAB to work out frequencies and couplings. Five: the waveguides are redrawn by hand and the loop goes back to simulation, again and again. And six: the chip goes to the cleanroom, is cooled down and measured, and that is the first time anyone sees how it really behaves.
